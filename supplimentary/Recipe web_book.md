## **Mint Lime Drink (Baby’s Favourite)**

A refreshing mint lime cooler with a gentle ginger twist- prefect for hot days  
**Easy • 10 mins • Drink • Veg**

### **Ingredients**

* Fresh mint leaves – 10 to 25  
* 1 lime (or lemon)  
* A small piece of ginger  
* 4 tbsp sugar (adjust to taste)  
* 3 cups cold water or soda  
* Ice cubes, as needed  
* A pinch of salt (optional)

### **Method**

1. Lightly crush mint leaves with sugar, ginger, and a little lime juice using a blender.  
2. Squeeze the full lime into the mixture.  
3. Add cold water (or soda for a fizzy version) and stir well.  
4. Strain if you prefer a smooth drink, or keep the crushed mint for extra flavor.  
5. Add ice cubes and a pinch of salt if using.  
6. Stir and serve chilled.

### **Note**

* Adjust sugar and lime based on how sweet or tangy you prefer.  
* Soda gives a refreshing fizzy version, while water keeps it light and simple.

## **Falooda Milkshake 🍓**

A creamy and delicious strawberry milkshake with soft vermicelli and crunchy cashew nuts — perfect for a refreshing treat\!

**Easy • 15 mins • Drink • Vegetarian**

### **Ingredients**

* ½ cup vermicelli  
* 2 cups water  
* 300 ml strawberry ice cream (approximately 3 scoops)  
* ½ litre milk, chilled  
* 6 tbsp powdered sugar  
* Chopped cashew nuts, for garnish

### **Method**

1. Boil the water in a pan.  
2. Add the vermicelli and cook until soft.  
3. Drain any excess water and allow the vermicelli to cool.  
4. Blend the cooked vermicelli in a blender.  
5. Add the chilled milk, strawberry ice cream and powdered sugar. Blend until smooth.  
6. Pour into glasses.  
7. Garnish with chopped cashew nuts and serve chilled.

### **Note**

* Adjust the sugar according to your taste.  
* For a thicker and creamier milkshake, reduce the amount of milk or add an extra scoop of strawberry ice cream.

## **Spiced Buttermilk 🌿**

A refreshing and savoury buttermilk flavoured with ginger, green chilli, shallots and curry leaves — a perfect cooling drink for hot days\!

**Easy • 10 mins • Drink • Vegetarian**

### **Ingredients**

* 1 small piece of ginger, chopped  
* 1 large green chilli (adjust according to your spice level)  
* 4–5 small onions (shallots)  
* 2 lemon leaves (optional)  
* ½ litre curd  
* Salt, to taste  
* A few curry leaves, chopped  
* 3 cups water

### **Method**

1. Crush the ginger, green chilli and shallots in the small jar of a mixer.  
2. Add the curd to a large bowl and whisk until smooth.  
3. Add the crushed ingredients and chopped curry leaves to the curd.  
4. Add salt to taste and mix well.  
5. Gradually add the water while whisking until the buttermilk reaches your desired consistency.  
6. Serve chilled or at room temperature.

### **Note**

* Adjust the green chilli and salt according to your taste.  
* For a more refreshing flavour, chill the buttermilk before serving.  
* Lemon leaves are optional but add a lovely aroma.

## **Indian Tea ☕ (Chai)**

A comforting and aromatic Indian tea made with tea dust, milk and sugar, with an optional hint of cardamom for extra flavour.

**Easy • 10 mins • Drink • Vegetarian**

### **Ingredients (2 Cups)**

* 2 cups water  
* 1 cup milk  
* 2–3 tsp sugar (adjust to taste)  
* 2 tsp tea dust  
* A pinch of cardamom powder (optional)

### **Method**

1. Bring the water to a boil in a pan.  
2. Add the tea dust and let it boil for 2 minutes.  
3. Pour in the milk and bring the mixture to a boil again.  
4. Add the sugar and simmer for 1–2 minutes.  
5. Add a pinch of cardamom powder, if desired, and stir well.  
6. Strain the tea into cups and serve hot.

### **Note**

* Adjust the sugar and tea dust according to your taste.  
* For a stronger tea, allow the tea dust to boil a little longer before adding the milk.  
* Cardamom powder is optional but adds a lovely aroma and flavour.

## **Lemonade 🍋**

A simple and refreshing lemon drink with the perfect balance of sweetness and tanginess — ideal for hot days\!

**Easy • 5 mins • Drink • Vegetarian**

### **Ingredients (1 Glass)**

* 3 tbsp lemon juice  
* 2 tbsp sugar  
* ¼ cup hot water  
* ½ cup cold water  
* Ice cubes, as needed  
* 1 lemon wedge, for garnish

### **Method**

1. Add the sugar to the hot water and stir until completely dissolved.  
2. Add the lemon juice and mix well.  
3. Fill a tall glass with ice cubes.  
4. Pour the lemon-sugar mixture into the glass.  
5. Add ½ cup cold water and stir well.  
6. Garnish the rim of the glass with a lemon wedge and serve immediately.

### **Note**

* Adjust the sugar and lemon juice according to your taste.  
* For extra refreshment, use chilled water and plenty of ice cubes.

## **Lime Juice 🍋**

A refreshing lime drink with a hint of ginger, cardamom and coconut — a delightful combination of sweet, tangy and aromatic flavours\!

**Easy • 10 mins • Drink • Vegetarian**

### **Ingredients**

* 1 lime  
* 1 small piece of ginger  
* 1 cardamom pod  
* ¼ cup sugar  
* 2 cups water  
* 1 tsp grated coconut  
* Ice cubes, as needed

### **Method**

1. Add the sugar and 1 cup of water to a blender and blend until the sugar dissolves.  
2. Add the ginger, cardamom and grated coconut. Blend well.  
3. Add the remaining 1 cup of water and blend again.  
4. Squeeze the lime juice into the mixture and blend briefly.  
5. Strain the mixture through a sieve.  
6. Pour into glasses, add ice cubes and serve chilled.

### **Note**

* Adjust the sugar according to your taste.  
* Blend briefly after adding the lime juice to preserve its fresh flavour.  
* Serve immediately for the best taste and aroma.

## **Pomegranate & Green Gram Sprouts Salad**

**A fresh, crunchy salad packed with sprouts, vegetables, and sweet pomegranate.**  
**Easy • 15 mins • Veg • Healthy Salad**

### **Ingredients**

* 1 cup green gram sprouts  
* 1/2 carrot, finely chopped  
* 1/2 cup pomegranate seeds  
* 1 small onion, finely chopped  
* 1 small cucumber, chopped  
* 1/2 red bell pepper, chopped  
* 1–2 green chillies, finely chopped  
* 2 tbsp fresh coriander leaves  
* 1 tbsp lemon juice  
* 2 tsp olive oil  
* 1/2 tsp black pepper powder  
* 1 tsp chaat masala (optional)  
* Salt to taste

### **Method**

1. Soak green gram overnight. Drain in the morning and leave it to sprout until evening.  
2. Lightly blanch the sprouts in boiling water for 2 minutes, then drain completely and let cool.  
3. Just before serving, chop all vegetables.  
4. In a bowl, combine sprouts, carrot, onion, cucumber, bell pepper, green chillies, and pomegranate seeds.  
5. Add chopped coriander leaves.  
6. In a small bowl, mix olive oil, lemon juice, black pepper, chaat masala, and salt.  
7. Pour the dressing over the salad and toss well.  
8. Serve immediately.

### **Note**

* You can skip blanching if you prefer fully raw sprouts (ensure they are fresh and clean).  
* Adjust chilli and lemon juice based on your taste.

## **Tomato Soup 🍅**

A warm and comforting tomato soup made with fresh vegetables, black pepper and butter. Choose coriander leaves for an Indian flavour or basil leaves for an Italian touch.

**Easy • 30 mins • Soup • Vegetarian**

### **Ingredients**

* 3 large tomatoes, chopped  
* 1 small piece of red bell pepper, chopped  
* 1 small carrot, chopped  
* 3 garlic cloves, chopped  
* 1 green chilli  
* A few coriander leaves or basil leaves  
* 1 medium onion, chopped  
* 1/2 tbsp black pepper (adjust to taste)  
* 1 tbsp butter  
* 1 tbsp cornflour  
* 4 cups water  
* Salt, to taste

### **Method**

1. Chop all the vegetables and place them in a large saucepan.  
2. Add 4 cups of water and black pepper.  
3. Boil until all the vegetables become soft.  
4. Add coriander leaves for an Indian flavour or basil leaves for an Italian flavour.  
5. Allow the mixture to cool, then blend until smooth.  
6. Strain the blended mixture through a sieve for a smooth soup.  
7. In the same empty saucepan, melt the butter.  
8. Add the cornflour and sauté for a minute or until the raw smell disappears. Avoid browning it.  
9. Gradually add the strained soup, stirring continuously to prevent lumps.  
10. Add salt to taste and bring the soup to a boil.  
11. Simmer until the soup thickens slightly.  
12. Serve hot.

### **Note**

* Adjust the black pepper and green chilli according to your preferred spice level.  
* For a smoother, creamier texture, add a little milk or fresh cream before serving, if desired.

## **Egg Drop Chicken Soup 🍲**

A warm and comforting chicken soup with tender shredded chicken and silky egg ribbons in a flavourful broth — perfect for a light and nourishing meal.

**Easy • 20 mins • Soup • Non-Vegetarian**

### **Ingredients**

* 1 cup cooked shredded chicken (boiled or leftover)  
* 4 cups chicken stock (homemade or store-bought)  
* 2 eggs  
* 1 tbsp cornflour (cornstarch)  
* 2 tbsp water (to mix with cornflour)  
* 1 tsp ginger, finely chopped or grated  
* 1–2 garlic cloves, minced (optional)  
* Salt, to taste  
* White pepper or black pepper, to taste  
* Chopped spring onions, for garnish

### **Method**

1. Pour the chicken stock into a pot and bring it to a gentle boil.  
2. Add the ginger and garlic, if using, and simmer for 2–3 minutes.  
3. Add the shredded chicken and cook for another 2–3 minutes.  
4. Mix the cornflour with 2 tbsp water until smooth.  
5. Slowly pour the cornflour mixture into the soup while stirring continuously. Cook until the soup becomes slightly silky.  
6. Beat the eggs well in a bowl.  
7. Reduce the heat to low and slowly pour the beaten eggs into the soup in a thin stream, gently stirring in one direction to create silky egg ribbons.  
8. Add salt and white pepper or black pepper to taste.  
9. Turn off the heat.  
10. Garnish with chopped spring onions and serve hot.

### **Note**

* Pour the beaten eggs slowly to create delicate, silky ribbons.  
* Adjust the cornflour according to your preferred soup consistency.  
* Taste the chicken stock before adding salt, as store-bought stock may already be salty.  
* Ensure the chicken is thoroughly cooked and the eggs are safely cooked before serving.

## **Mushroom Soup 🍄**

A warm and creamy mushroom soup made with fresh mushrooms, garlic and onions, finished with a hint of pepper. Perfect for a light and comforting meal.

**Easy • 20 mins • Soup • Vegetarian**

### **Ingredients**

* 100 g mushrooms, chopped  
* 1 tbsp butter  
* 3 garlic cloves, chopped  
* ½ onion, chopped  
* Spring onions, chopped  
* 2 cups water  
* 1 tbsp cornflour  
* ½ tsp pepper  
* Salt, to taste  
* Water, as needed to mix the cornflour

  ### **Method**

1. Heat a large saucepan and melt the butter.  
2. Add the chopped garlic and sauté briefly.  
3. Add the chopped onion and sauté until translucent.  
4. Add the chopped mushrooms and spring onions. Sauté for about 3 minutes.  
5. Add salt, pepper and 2 cups of water.  
6. Cover and cook for about 10 minutes, until the mushrooms are tender.  
7. Mix the cornflour with a little water until smooth.  
8. Add the cornflour mixture to the soup, stirring continuously, and bring to a boil.  
9. Switch off the heat and allow the soup to cool slightly.  
10. Use a hand blender to blend until smooth.  
11. Reheat gently if needed and serve hot.

    ### **Note**

* Adjust the thickness by adding a little water or stock.  
* For a richer flavour, add a splash of milk or fresh cream before serving, if desired.

## **Broccoli Soup 🥦**

A smooth and nutritious broccoli soup with potato, garlic and onions, enriched with a little butter and olive oil.

**Easy • 25 mins • Soup • Vegetarian**

### **Ingredients**

* 1 broccoli, cut into florets  
* 1 small potato, sliced  
* 3 garlic cloves, chopped  
* ¼ of a large onion, chopped  
* ½ vegetable stock cube (such as Maggi vegetable stock cube)  
* 3 cups water  
* Salt and pepper, to taste  
* 2 tsp olive oil  
* 1 tsp butter

### **Method**

1. Add the sliced potato, vegetable stock cube and 2 cups of water to a pressure cooker.  
2. Pressure-cook until the potato is soft, about one whistle, depending on your cooker.  
3. Heat the olive oil and butter in a large pan.  
4. Add the chopped onion and sauté until translucent.  
5. Add the garlic and stir for about a minute.  
6. Add the broccoli florets and sauté until they begin to soften.  
7. Transfer the broccoli mixture to a blender.  
8. Add the cooked potato along with its cooking liquid and blend until smooth.  
9. Pour the blended mixture back into the pan.  
10. Add some of the remaining water if needed to achieve your preferred consistency.  
11. Bring to a boil and simmer for 2 minutes.  
12. Season with salt and pepper to taste.  
13. Serve hot.

### **Note**

* Do not overcook the broccoli, as this helps preserve its fresh flavour and colour.  
* Add water gradually to control the thickness of the soup.  
* Taste before adding salt because the vegetable stock cube already contains salt.

## **Urad Dal (Black Gram) Vada**

Crispy golden vadas made from soaked urad dal and mild spices.  
**Medium • 40 mins • Veg • Snack • Fried**

### **Ingredients**

* 200 grams urad dal  
* 1 tsp cumin seeds  
* A pinch of hing (asafoetida powder)  
* 1 inch ginger, finely chopped  
* Curry leaves, chopped  
* 1 tsp pepper powder (or to taste)  
* Salt, to taste  
* Oil, for deep frying

### **Method**

1. Soak urad dal for 6 hours or overnight.  
2. Drain and blend into a coarse, thick paste. Add cumin seeds and blend lightly.  
3. Transfer the batter to a bowl. Add pepper powder, hing, and salt. Mix well.  
4. Add chopped ginger and curry leaves. Mix with your hands until the batter becomes light and fluffy.  
5. Heat oil in a frying pan for deep frying.  
6. Shape the batter into small donut-like vadas.  
7. Carefully drop into hot oil and fry until golden brown.  
8. Remove and drain on paper towels. Serve hot.

### **Note**

* Do not add too much water while grinding; a thick batter helps shape the vadas.  
* Wet your hands slightly while shaping to prevent sticking.  
* Fry on medium heat to ensure even cooking inside.

## **Paruppu Vada (Dal Vada) 🧆**

A traditional South Indian crispy snack made with coarsely ground dal, green chillies, ginger and curry leaves. Crispy on the outside and deliciously flavourful inside\!

**Easy • 40 mins \+ 3 hours soaking • Snack • Vegetarian**

### **Ingredients**

* ½ kg peas dal (vada paruppu)  
* 15 green chillies  
* 2 square pieces of ginger  
* Salt, to taste  
* Curry leaves, chopped  
* Oil, for deep-frying

*Note: You can also use chana dal (Bengal gram) or other suitable lentils, but peas dal is preferred for this recipe.*

### **Method**

1. Wash the dal thoroughly and soak it in water for 3 hours.  
2. Drain the soaked dal completely.  
3. Grind the green chillies and ginger coarsely in a blender. Transfer to a mixing bowl.  
4. Add the drained dal to the blender and grind coarsely. Do not add excess water.  
5. Transfer the ground dal to the mixing bowl.  
6. Add the chopped curry leaves and salt. Mix everything well.  
7. Wet your hands and take a small, lemon-sized portion of the mixture.  
8. Shape it into a ball and flatten it gently on your palm to form a vada.  
9. Heat the oil in a deep pan over medium heat.  
10. Carefully slide the vadas into the hot oil. Fry 4–5 at a time, depending on the size of the pan.  
11. Fry over medium heat, flipping occasionally, until both sides are golden brown and crispy.  
12. Remove and drain on kitchen paper. Serve hot.

### **Note**

* Drain the dal thoroughly to prevent the mixture from becoming too wet.  
* Grind the dal coarsely for the traditional texture.  
* Adjust the green chillies according to your spice preference.  
* Avoid overcrowding the pan, as this can lower the oil temperature and make the vadas less crispy.

## **Samosa 🥟**

A popular Indian snack with a crispy, golden pastry filled with spiced potatoes, green peas and aromatic Indian spices. Perfect for tea time or as a party snack\!

**Medium • 60 mins • Snack • Vegetarian**

### **Ingredients**

#### **For the Dough**

* 2 cups all-purpose flour (maida)  
* 4 tbsp oil or ghee  
* ½ tsp salt  
* Water, as needed for kneading

#### **For the Filling**

* 3 medium potatoes, boiled and mashed  
* ½ cup green peas (fresh or frozen)  
* 1 onion, finely chopped  
* 2 green chillies, chopped  
* 1 tsp grated ginger  
* 1 tsp cumin seeds  
* 1 tsp coriander powder  
* ½ tsp garam masala  
* ½ tsp turmeric powder  
* ½ tsp red chilli powder  
* Salt, to taste  
* 2 tbsp oil  
* Fresh coriander leaves, chopped  
* A little lemon juice or amchur (dry mango powder), optional  
* Oil, for deep-frying

### **Method**

#### **1\. Make the Dough**

1. Mix the flour and salt in a large bowl.  
2. Add the oil or ghee and rub it into the flour until the mixture resembles breadcrumbs.  
3. Add water gradually and knead into a firm, stiff dough. Do not make it too soft.  
4. Cover and let the dough rest for 20–30 minutes.

#### **2\. Prepare the Filling**

1. Heat 2 tbsp oil in a pan.  
2. Add the cumin seeds and allow them to crackle.  
3. Add the chopped onion, green chillies and ginger. Sauté until the onion softens.  
4. Add the green peas and cook for 2–3 minutes.  
5. Add the turmeric powder, red chilli powder, coriander powder and salt. Mix well.  
6. Add the mashed potatoes and combine thoroughly.  
7. Stir in the garam masala, chopped coriander leaves and lemon juice or amchur, if using.  
8. Remove from the heat and allow the filling to cool completely.

#### **3\. Shape the Samosas**

1. Divide the dough into small, equal-sized balls.  
2. Roll each ball into an oval shape.  
3. Cut the oval in half to make two semicircles.  
4. Take one semicircle and form a cone, sealing the straight edge with a little water.  
5. Fill the cone with the cooled potato mixture.  
6. Apply a little water to the edges and seal the top firmly.  
7. Repeat with the remaining dough and filling.

#### **4\. Fry the Samosas**

1. Heat oil in a deep pan over low to medium heat.  
2. Carefully add the samosas to the oil without overcrowding the pan.  
3. Fry slowly until they turn golden brown and crispy, turning occasionally for even cooking.  
4. Remove and drain on kitchen paper.  
5. Serve hot with mint chutney or tamarind chutney.

### **Tips for Perfect Samosas**

* **Firm dough:** A stiff dough helps create a crispy pastry.  
* **Cool filling:** Always let the potato filling cool completely before stuffing.  
* **Slow frying:** Start frying at a lower temperature so the pastry cooks evenly and becomes crisp.  
* **Seal properly:** Ensure all edges are tightly sealed to prevent the filling from escaping.  
* **Air-frying option:** Brush the samosas with oil and air-fry at 180°C for approximately 15–18 minutes, turning halfway through. Cooking time may vary by air fryer.

**Chicken Cutlets 🍗**

Crispy, golden chicken cutlets made with shredded chicken, mashed potatoes and aromatic Kerala spices. Perfect for tea time, parties or as a starter.

**Medium • 45 mins • Snack • Non-Vegetarian**

### **Ingredients**

* 300 g boneless chicken  
* 2 medium potatoes, boiled and mashed  
* 2 onions, finely chopped  
* 2 green chillies, chopped  
* 1 tbsp ginger-garlic paste  
* A few curry leaves, chopped  
* ¼ tsp turmeric powder  
* 1 tsp red chilli powder  
* ½ tsp black pepper powder  
* 1 tsp garam masala  
* Salt, to taste  
* A small handful of coriander leaves, chopped  
* 2 tbsp oil, for sautéing  
* A small piece of beetroot (optional, for colour)

  ### **For Coating**

* 2 eggs, beaten  
* Breadcrumbs, as needed

  ### **For Frying**

* Oil, as needed

  ### **Method**

  #### **1\. Cook and Shred the Chicken**

1. Cook the chicken with a little salt and turmeric powder by boiling or pressure-cooking until fully cooked.  
2. Allow it to cool slightly and shred it finely.

   #### **2\. Prepare the Masala Filling**

1. Heat 2 tbsp oil in a pan.  
2. Add the chopped onions and curry leaves. Sauté until golden.  
3. Add the ginger-garlic paste and cook until the raw smell disappears.  
4. Add the green chillies, turmeric powder, red chilli powder, black pepper and garam masala.  
5. Mix well, then add the shredded chicken.  
6. Cook until the mixture is dry.

   #### **3\. Combine with Potatoes**

1. Add the mashed potatoes and mix thoroughly.  
2. Add finely grated beetroot, if using, for a lovely colour.  
3. Add the chopped coriander leaves.  
4. Taste and adjust the salt and spices.  
5. Allow the mixture to cool completely.

   #### **4\. Shape the Cutlets**

1. Take small portions of the mixture.  
2. Shape them into oval or round patties.

   #### **5\. Coat the Cutlets**

1. Dip each cutlet in beaten egg.  
2. Roll it in breadcrumbs until evenly coated.

   #### **6\. Fry the Cutlets**

1. Heat oil over medium heat.  
2. Fry the cutlets until golden brown and crispy on both sides.  
3. Remove and drain on kitchen paper.

   ### **Air-Fryer Method**

1. Preheat the air fryer to 180°C for 3–5 minutes.  
2. Arrange the cutlets in the basket without overcrowding.  
3. Lightly brush or spray oil on both sides.  
4. Air-fry at 180°C for 12–15 minutes, flipping halfway through.  
5. Cook until golden and crispy.

   ### **Note**

* Ensure the chicken mixture is dry before shaping the cutlets.  
* Cool the filling completely to make shaping easier.  
* Cooking time may vary depending on the size and thickness of the cutlets.  
* vary by air fryer.

## **Vegetable Cutlets 🥕🥔**

Delicious, crispy vegetable cutlets made with mashed potatoes, colourful vegetables and aromatic spices. A perfect vegetarian snack for tea time or parties.

**Medium • 40 mins • Snack • Vegetarian**

### **Ingredients**

* 3 medium potatoes, boiled and mashed  
* 1 carrot, finely chopped  
* 8 green beans, finely chopped  
* ½ cup green peas  
* 2 onions, finely chopped  
* 2 green chillies, chopped  
* 1 tsp finely chopped ginger and garlic  
* A few curry leaves, chopped  
* 1 small piece of beetroot (optional, for colour)  
* Finely chopped cauliflower (optional)

### **Spices**

* ¼ tsp turmeric powder  
* 1 tsp red chilli powder  
* ½ tsp black pepper powder  
* 1 tsp garam masala  
* Salt, to taste  
* A handful of chopped coriander leaves

### **For Coating**

* 2 eggs, beaten, or maida slurry for an eggless version  
* Breadcrumbs, as needed

### **For Cooking**

* 2 tbsp oil, for sautéing  
* Oil, for frying or brushing

### **Method**

#### **1\. Cook the Vegetables**

1. Steam or boil the carrot, beans and green peas until just tender. Do not overcook.  
2. Add finely chopped cauliflower, if desired.  
3. Add a small piece of beetroot for colour, if using.  
4. Drain all the water completely and set the vegetables aside.

#### **2\. Prepare the Masala**

1. Heat 2 tbsp oil in a pan.  
2. Add the chopped onions and curry leaves. Sauté until golden.  
3. Add the ginger, garlic and green chillies.  
4. Add the turmeric powder, red chilli powder, black pepper, garam masala and salt.  
5. Mix well and cook briefly.

#### **3\. Prepare the Filling**

1. Add the cooked vegetables to the masala.

## **Dhokla 🍋**

A soft, fluffy Gujarati steamed snack made with gram flour and topped with a tangy, lightly sweet mustard-seed tempering. Perfect for breakfast, tea-time, or as a light snack.

**Easy • 40–45 minutes • Vegetarian • Serves 4**

### **Ingredients**

**For the dhokla batter**

* Gram flour (besan) – 1½ cups  
* Salt – ½ tsp  
* Sugar – ½ tsp (optional)  
* Turmeric powder – ½ tsp  
* Eno fruit salt – 1 tsp  
* Water – as needed  
* Oil – for greasing the cake tin

**For the tempering syrup**

* Oil – 1 tsp  
* Mustard seeds – ½ tsp  
* Green chillies – 5, slit lengthwise  
* Curry leaves – a few  
* Water – ½ cup  
* Salt – ½ tsp  
* Sugar – 2 tsp (optional)  
* Lemon juice – juice of ½ lemon  
* Coriander leaves – chopped, for garnish  
* Grated coconut – for garnish (optional)

### **Method**

**1\. Prepare the batter**

1. Add the gram flour, salt, sugar, and turmeric powder to a bowl.  
2. Gradually add water and whisk until you get a smooth, lump-free batter with a flowing consistency. It should not be too thick.  
3. Let the batter rest for about 10 minutes.  
4. Grease a cake tin with oil.

**2\. Steam the dhokla**

1. Add water to a pressure cooker or large pot and place a steaming stand inside.  
2. Bring the water to a boil.  
3. Just before steaming, add the Eno fruit salt to the batter and gently mix in one direction. The batter will become light and bubbly.  
4. Immediately pour the batter into the greased cake tin.  
5. Carefully place the tin on the stand inside the pot.  
6. Cover and steam over medium-low heat for approximately 20 minutes. If using a pressure cooker, do not use the pressure regulator or whistle.  
7. Insert a toothpick into the centre. If it comes out clean, the dhokla is cooked.  
8. Remove the tin carefully and allow the dhokla to cool.

**3\. Prepare the tempering syrup**

1. Heat 1 tsp oil in a small pan.  
2. Add the mustard seeds and allow them to splutter.  
3. Add the slit green chillies and curry leaves. Sauté briefly.  
4. Pour in the water and add the salt and sugar, if using.  
5. Bring to a boil, then turn off the heat.  
6. Add the lemon juice and chopped coriander leaves. Stir well.

**4\. Assemble and serve**

1. Remove the cooled dhokla from the tin and cut it into squares.  
2. Pour the prepared tempering syrup evenly over the dhokla.  
3. Allow it to rest for a few minutes so the dhokla absorbs the flavours.  
4. Garnish with grated coconut and additional chopped coriander leaves, if desired.

### **Notes**

* Add the Eno only just before steaming to help the dhokla become soft and fluffy.  
* Do not overmix the batter after adding Eno, and steam it immediately.

* Adjust the green chillies, lemon juice, and sugar to suit your taste.

## **Tandoori Chicken 🍗**

Juicy, smoky and flavourful tandoori chicken marinated in hung curd, homemade tandoori masala, ginger, garlic and spices. Slow-cooked until tender with a delicious roasted finish. Serve with mint chutney for an authentic Indian-style meal\!

**Medium • 7 hours 30 mins (including marination) • Appetites       • Non-Vegetarian**

### **Ingredients**

#### **For the Marinade**

* ½ large onion, chopped  
* 3 large garlic cloves, chopped  
* 1-inch piece of ginger, chopped  
* 2 green chillies, chopped  
* 2 tsp curd, for grinding  
* 4 tbsp hung curd (thick curd, strained to remove excess water)  
* 2 tbsp homemade tandoori masala powder  
* ½ tsp salt

#### **For the Chicken**

* 2 large chicken thigh-and-leg pieces, kept together or cut in half  
* ¾ tsp salt  
* Juice of 1 lemon  
* Ghee or oil, as needed  
* 1 tbsp cornflour (optional, to thicken the marinade)

#### **For the Smoky Flavour (Optional)**

* 1 small piece of charcoal  
* A little ghee, for drizzling

### **Method**

#### **Step 1: Prepare the Onion Paste**

1. Add the chopped onion, garlic, ginger and green chillies to a mixer jar.  
2. Add 2 tsp curd and grind everything into a smooth paste.

#### **Step 2: Prepare the Marinade**

1. Place the hung curd in a bowl.  
2. Add the homemade tandoori masala powder, ground onion paste and ½ tsp salt.  
3. Mix well to form a thick marinade.

#### **Step 3: Marinate the Chicken**

1. Make deep slits on both sides of the chicken pieces to help the marinade penetrate.  
2. Pat the chicken dry with a paper towel.  
3. Rub the chicken with ¾ tsp salt and the juice of 1 lemon.  
4. Leave for 10 minutes, then drain any excess liquid.  
5. Add the curd marinade and massage it thoroughly into the chicken.  
6. Add 1 tbsp cornflour if needed to thicken the marinade.  
7. Cover and refrigerate for at least 6 hours.

#### **Step 4: Add the Smoky Flavour (Optional)**

1. Heat a small piece of charcoal over a flame until it is glowing hot.  
2. Place the hot charcoal in a small heatproof bowl.  
3. Put the small bowl inside the bowl containing the marinated chicken, ensuring the charcoal does not touch the chicken.  
4. Drizzle a little ghee over the charcoal and immediately cover the chicken bowl.  
5. Leave covered for about 5 minutes to infuse the smoky flavour, then carefully remove the charcoal bowl.

#### **Step 5: Cook the Chicken in a Pan**

1. Heat a little ghee in a heavy-bottomed pan.  
2. Place the marinated chicken pieces in the pan and cover with a lid.  
3. Cook over medium-low heat for about 20 minutes.  
4. Open the lid and carefully flip the chicken.  
5. Spread some of the masala from the pan over the chicken pieces.  
6. Cover and cook for another 20 minutes, or until the chicken is cooked through.  
7. Flip the chicken again and dab a little ghee over the pieces.  
8. Roast uncovered for another 5 minutes, turning as needed, until the chicken develops a lightly charred finish.

#### **Step 6: Serve**

1. Transfer the tandoori chicken to a serving plate.  
2. Serve hot with mint chutney, lemon wedges and sliced onions.

### **Oven Method**

1. Preheat the oven to 200°C.  
2. Arrange the marinated chicken on a foil-lined baking tray or oven-safe rack.  
3. Brush with a little ghee or oil.  
4. Bake for approximately 35–45 minutes, turning halfway through and brushing with ghee or oil as needed.  
5. Grill briefly at the end for a charred finish, watching carefully to prevent burning.  
6. Ensure the thickest part of the chicken reaches an internal temperature of 74°C before serving.

### **Notes**

* **Hung curd is important:** It helps the marinade cling to the chicken and prevents it from becoming too watery.  
* **Longer marination:** Refrigerating for at least 6 hours gives the chicken a deeper flavour.  
* **Keep it juicy:** Baste with ghee or oil during cooking to prevent the surface from drying out.  
* **Charcoal safety:** Use a heatproof bowl, ensure good ventilation and never leave burning charcoal unattended indoors. Avoid using a cinnamon stick as a substitute for charcoal because it can burn unpredictably.

4. Heat a tawa over medium heat and cook each pathiri briefly on both sides. They should be only partially cooked.

**4\. Prepare the chicken masala**

1. Heat oil in a pan.  
2. Add the onions and curry leaves and sauté until the onions turn golden.  
3. Add the crushed ginger, garlic, and green chillies. Sauté until fragrant.  
4. Add the red chilli powder, coriander powder, turmeric powder, pepper powder, garam masala, and salt. Mix well.  
5. Add the shredded chicken and stir until the spices are evenly distributed.  
6. Cook until the filling is fairly dry, then add the chopped coriander leaves.  
7. Remove from the heat and allow the filling to cool slightly.

**5\. Prepare the egg mixture**

1. Beat the eggs with milk, salt, and black pepper until well combined.

**6\. Assemble the chatti pathiri**

1. Grease a deep pan with ghee. Choose a pan with a base approximately the same size as the pathiris.  
2. Dip one pathiri into the egg mixture and place it at the bottom of the pan.  
3. Spread a layer of chicken masala over the pathiri.  
4. Dip the second pathiri into the egg mixture and place it on top.  
5. Spread another layer of chicken masala and arrange some slices of boiled egg over it.  
6. Continue layering the pathiris and chicken filling, adding boiled egg slices between the layers as desired.  
7. Finish with a pathiri on top and pour the remaining egg mixture evenly over the layers.

**7\. Cook the chatti pathiri**

**Stovetop method**

1. Cover the pan and cook over low heat for 15–20 minutes, or until the egg mixture is set and the layers are cooked through.  
2. Carefully flip the chatti pathiri using a plate, if necessary.  
3. Cook the other side until golden and fully set.

**Oven method**

1. Preheat the oven to 180°C.  
2. Bake for 25–30 minutes, or until the egg mixture is set and the top is golden brown.

### **Notes**

* Keep the chicken filling fairly dry to prevent the layers from becoming soggy.  
* Cook the pathiris only partially before assembling, as they will cook further during the final cooking stage.  
* Use low heat on the stovetop to ensure the egg mixture sets without burning the bottom.  
* Allow the chatti pathiri to rest for 5–10 minutes before cutting it into wedges.  
* Serve warm with mint chutney, yoghurt, or your favourite salad.

**Tamarind Rice (Puliyodarai) 🍋**

A traditional South Indian rice dish with a tangy tamarind gravy, roasted peanuts, lentils, and aromatic spices.

**Medium • 40–50 minutes • Vegetarian**

### **Ingredients**

**For the rice**

* Rice – 1 cup  
* Water – 2 cups  
* Salt – as needed

**For the tamarind gravy**

* Tamarind – 1 lemon-sized ball  
* Water – as needed to extract approximately 1 cup of tamarind juice  
* Oil – 1 tbsp  
* Mustard seeds – ½ tsp  
* Split chickpeas (chana dal) – 1 tbsp  
* Raw peanuts – 2 tbsp  
* Fenugreek seeds – a pinch  
* Dried red chillies – 3  
* Green chillies – 2, chopped  
* Curry leaves – 1 sprig  
* Asafoetida (hing) – a pinch  
* Turmeric powder – ¼ tsp  
* Red chilli powder – ¼ tsp  
* Coriander powder – 1 tsp  
* Cumin powder – 1 tsp  
* Roasted sesame seed powder – 1 tbsp (optional)  
* Salt – to taste

### **Method**

**1\. Cook the rice**

1. Wash the rice thoroughly.  
2. Add the rice and 2 cups of water to a pressure cooker.  
3. Cook for one whistle, or until the rice is tender.  
4. Allow the pressure to release safely, then fluff the rice and set aside.

**2\. Prepare the tamarind juice**

1. Soak the tamarind in warm water until softened.  
2. Squeeze out the pulp and strain to obtain approximately 1 cup of tamarind juice.

**3\. Prepare the tamarind gravy**

1. Heat the oil in a pan.  
2. Add the dried red chillies, mustard seeds, and fenugreek seeds.  
3. When the mustard seeds splutter, add the chana dal and peanuts. Fry until the dal turns golden and the peanuts are lightly roasted.  
4. Add the curry leaves, green chillies, and asafoetida.  
5. Add the turmeric powder, red chilli powder, coriander powder, and cumin powder. Stir briefly.  
6. Pour in the tamarind juice and add salt.  
7. Simmer for 10–12 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the gravy thickens and the oil begins to separate.  
8. Add the roasted sesame seed powder, if using, and cook for another 5–10 minutes.

**4\. Combine and serve**

1. Add the tamarind gravy gradually to the cooked rice.  
2. Mix gently until the rice is evenly coated.  
3. Allow the rice to rest briefly so the flavours can develop.  
4. Serve warm or at room temperature.

### **Notes**

* Adjust the tamarind juice according to how tangy you like the rice.  
* Add the gravy gradually so the rice does not become too wet.  
* Roasted sesame seed powder adds a nutty flavour but is optional.  
* The rice tastes even better after resting for a short time, allowing the tamarind and spices to blend.

**Vegetable Biryani 🥕🍚**

A fragrant and flavourful biryani made with basmati or Kerala jeerakasala rice, mixed vegetables, aromatic spices, fresh herbs, and ghee. Slow-cooked on dum for a delicious Kerala-style flavour.

**Medium • 60–75 minutes • Vegetarian • Serves 4**

### **Ingredients**

**For the rice**

* Basmati rice – 2 cups (or jeerakasala rice for authentic Kerala taste)  
* Water – as needed  
* Salt – to taste  
* Whole spices – cloves (4), cardamom (4), cinnamon (1-inch piece), bay leaves (2), and star anise (1, optional)

**For the vegetable masala**

* Mixed vegetables – 2 cups (carrot, beans, potato, peas, and cauliflower)  
* Onion – 3 large, thinly sliced  
* Tomato – 2 medium, chopped  
* Green chillies – 4  
* Ginger – 1-inch piece  
* Garlic – 8 cloves  
* Turmeric powder – ½ tsp  
* Red chilli powder – 1 tsp  
* Coriander powder – 1 tsp  
* Garam masala – 1 tsp  
* Black pepper powder – ½ tsp  
* Curd (yoghurt) – ¼ cup  
* Mint leaves – 1 cup  
* Coriander leaves – 1 cup  
* Lemon juice – 1 tbsp  
* Ghee – 3 tbsp  
* Oil – as needed  
* Salt – to taste

**For the garnish**

* Fried onions  
* Fried cashews – 2 tbsp  
* Fried raisins – 2 tbsp  
* Extra mint and coriander leaves  
* A pinch of turmeric mixed with 1 tbsp water, for colour

### **Method**

**1\. Cook the rice**

1. Bring a large pot of water to a boil. Add salt and the whole spices.  
2. Add the washed rice and cook until approximately 70% done.  
3. Drain the rice and remove the whole spices. Set aside.

**2\. Prepare the ginger-garlic paste**

1. Grind the ginger, garlic, and green chillies into a coarse paste.

**3\. Prepare the vegetable masala**

1. Heat oil and some ghee in a heavy-bottomed pan.  
2. Fry one sliced onion until golden brown. Remove and reserve for the garnish.  
3. Add the remaining onions and sauté until soft and translucent.  
4. Add the ground paste and sauté until fragrant.  
5. Add the tomatoes and cook until soft.  
6. Add the turmeric, chilli powder, coriander powder, pepper powder, and salt. Mix well.  
7. Add the mixed vegetables and cook for 5–7 minutes.  
8. Add the curd and cook until the vegetables are tender but not mushy.  
9. Add the garam masala, mint leaves, and coriander leaves. Mix well.

**4\. Layer the biryani**

1. Spread the vegetable masala evenly in a heavy-bottomed pot.  
2. Layer the partially cooked rice over the masala.  
3. Sprinkle the fried onions, cashews, raisins, mint, and coriander leaves over the rice.  
4. Drizzle the lemon juice and remaining ghee over the top.  
5. Sprinkle the turmeric-water mixture over the rice for colour.

**5\. Dum cooking**

1. Cover the pot tightly with a lid.  
2. Place a tawa underneath the pot and cook over very low heat for 15–20 minutes.  
3. Turn off the heat and let the biryani rest for 5–10 minutes before gently mixing and serving.

### **Notes**

* Jeerakasala rice gives the biryani a traditional Kerala flavour.  
* Do not fully cook the rice during the first stage, as it will continue cooking during dum.  
* Cut the vegetables into similar-sized pieces so they cook evenly.  
* Adjust the green chillies and chilli powder to your taste.

## **Fish Biryani 🐟**

A delicious Kerala-style fish biryani prepared with firm fish, aromatic spices, fresh herbs, and fragrant rice, finished with a slow dum-cooking process.

**Medium • 60–75 minutes • Non-vegetarian • Serves 4**

### **Ingredients**

**For the rice**

* Basmati rice – 2 cups (or jeerakasala rice for Kerala style)  
* Water – as needed  
* Salt – to taste  
* Bay leaf – 1  
* Lemon juice – 1 tsp

**For the fish**

* Boneless fish – 500 g  
* Turmeric powder – ¼ tsp  
* Red chilli powder – 1 tsp  
* Salt – to taste  
* Lemon juice – 1 tsp  
* Oil – for shallow frying or air frying

*Suitable fish: seer fish, kingfish, tilapia, basa, or another firm white fish. Frozen fish can also be used.*

**For the fish masala**

* Onion – 3 large, thinly sliced  
* Tomato – 2 medium, chopped  
* Green chillies – 4  
* Ginger – 1-inch piece  
* Garlic – 8–10 cloves  
* Turmeric powder – ½ tsp  
* Red chilli powder – 1½ tsp  
* Coriander powder – 1 tsp  
* Black pepper powder – ½ tsp  
* Garam masala – ½ tsp  
* Curd (yoghurt) – 2 tbsp  
* Mint leaves – 1 cup  
* Coriander leaves – 1 cup  
* Ghee – 3 tbsp  
* Oil – as needed  
* Salt – to taste  
* Whole spices – cloves (3), cardamom (3), cinnamon (1-inch piece), bay leaves (2), and fennel seeds (½ tsp)

**For the garnish**

* Fried onions  
* Cashews  
* Raisins  
* Extra mint and coriander leaves  
* Lemon juice – 1 tbsp  
* A pinch of turmeric mixed with 1 tbsp water, for colour

### **Method**

**1\. Thaw and marinate the fish**

1. If using frozen fish, thaw it completely and pat it dry.  
2. Marinate the fish with ¼ tsp turmeric powder, 1 tsp red chilli powder, salt, and 1 tsp lemon juice.  
3. Leave to marinate for 15–20 minutes.  
4. Lightly shallow-fry the fish until just cooked, or air-fry it until nearly cooked. Set aside.

**2\. Cook the rice**

1. Bring a pot of water to a boil with salt and the bay leaf.  
2. Add the washed rice and 1 tsp lemon juice.  
3. Cook until the rice is approximately 70% done.  
4. Drain and set aside.

**3\. Prepare the masala**

1. Grind the ginger, garlic, green chillies, and whole spices(no bay leaves) into a paste.  
2. Heat oil and some ghee in a heavy-bottomed pot.  
3. Fry one sliced onion until golden brown and set aside for the garnish.  
4. Fry the cashews until golden, then fry the raisins until they puff up. Set both aside with the fried onions.  
5. Add the remaining onions to the pot and sauté until soft and translucent.  
6. Add the ground paste and sauté until fragrant.  
7. Add the tomatoes and cook until soft.  
8. Add the turmeric, remaining chilli powder, coriander powder, pepper powder, garam masala, and salt. Mix well.  
9. Add the curd and cook until the masala thickens and the oil begins to separate.  
10. Add the mint and coriander leaves.

**4\. Add the fish**

1. Gently place the fried fish into the masala.  
2. Coat the fish lightly without breaking the pieces.  
3. Cook for another 2–3 minutes, then turn off the heat.

**5\. Layer the biryani**

1. Spread the fish masala evenly in the pot.  
2. Layer the partially cooked rice over the masala.  
3. Sprinkle the fried onions, cashews, raisins, mint, and coriander leaves between the rice layers and over the top.  
4. Drizzle the remaining ghee and lemon juice over the rice.  
5. Sprinkle the turmeric-water mixture over the top for colour.

**6\. Dum cooking**

1. Cover the pot tightly.  
2. Place a tawa underneath and cook over very low heat for 15–20 minutes.  
3. Turn off the heat and allow the biryani to rest for 5–10 minutes before serving.

### **Notes**

* Handle the fish gently to prevent it from breaking during layering.  
* Do not overcook the fish during frying because it will cook further during dum.  
* Ensure the fish is fully cooked before serving.  
* To make prawn biryani, replace the fish with 1 kg of prawns and adjust the cooking time so the prawns do not become rubbery.

* 1 tsp chilli powder

* Salt, to taste

* 1 tsp sugar (optional)

### **Notes**

# **Kerala Sambar**

A traditional Kerala-style sambar made with toor dal, mixed vegetables, roasted coconut-spice paste, and aromatic coconut oil seasoning. This recipe serves 8 people.

**Medium • 60 mins • Main Course • Veg • Serve hot**

### **Ingredients**

**For the dal**

* ½ cup toor dal (pigeon peas)  
* ¼ tsp turmeric powder  
* 1½ cups water  
* Salt to taste

**For the vegetables**

* 2 cups mixed vegetables, such as:  
  * 1 small potato  
  * ½ carrot  
  * 2 small brinjals  
  * 1 drumstick, cut into pieces  
* 4 ladies’ fingers (okra)  
* 1½ tomatoes, cut into large pieces  
* 6 small onions (shallots)  
* 2 green chillies, slit lengthwise  
* 1 lemon-sized ball of tamarind  
* 1 sprig curry leaves  
* 2 cups extra water, or as needed  
* ½ tsp turmeric powder  
* Salt to taste

**For roasting and grinding**

* 3½ tbsp coriander powder  
* ¼ tsp fenugreek seeds  
* ¼ tsp cumin seeds  
* 2½ tbsp red chilli powder  
* 1 tsp asafoetida powder  
* 3 tbsp grated coconut  
* 2 small onions (shallots)  
* 1 garlic clove  
* 1 sprig curry leaves  
* 1 tsp oil

**For seasoning**

* 2 tbsp coconut oil, or another oil of your choice  
* ¼ tsp mustard seeds  
* 10 fenugreek seeds  
* 2 dried red chillies, halved  
* 1 sprig curry leaves  
* 2 pinches asafoetida powder  
* Chopped coriander leaves, for garnish (optional)

### **Method**

**1\. Cook the dal**

1. Wash the toor dal thoroughly.  
2. Pressure-cook it with 1½ cups water, ¼ tsp turmeric powder, and salt for 2 whistles.  
3. Allow the pressure to release naturally, then open the cooker and set the cooked dal aside.

**2\. Cook the vegetables**

1. Soak the tamarind in warm water and extract the juice. Discard the pulp.  
2. Place the tamarind juice in a pressure cooker.  
3. Add the potato, carrot, brinjal, drumstick, and small onions.  
4. Add enough water to cook the vegetables, along with ½ tsp turmeric powder and salt.  
5. Pressure-cook for 1 whistle over medium heat. Allow the pressure to settle before opening.

**3\. Prepare the roasted coconut-spice paste**

1. Heat 1 tsp oil in a pan.  
2. Add the grated coconut, garlic, cumin seeds, fenugreek seeds, 2 small onions, and curry leaves. Roast until the coconut turns golden brown.  
3. Reduce the heat to very low and add the coriander powder. Roast until its raw smell disappears.  
4. Switch off the heat, add the red chilli powder, and mix well.  
5. Add the asafoetida powder and allow the mixture to cool.  
6. Grind the roasted mixture with a little water into a smooth paste.

**4\. Combine and simmer**

1. Transfer the cooked vegetables to a large pot if necessary. Add the cooked dal and mix well.  
2. Add the ladies’ fingers, slit green chillies, tomato pieces, remaining curry leaves, and extra water as needed.  
3. Bring to a boil.  
4. Add the ground coconut-spice paste and stir thoroughly.  
5. Simmer over medium-low heat for 15–20 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the vegetables are cooked and the sambar reaches the desired consistency.  
6. Taste and adjust the salt, sourness, and spice level.

**5\. Prepare the seasoning**

1. Heat 2 tbsp coconut oil in a small pan.  
2. Add the mustard seeds and allow them to splutter.  
3. Add the fenugreek seeds, halved dried red chillies, and curry leaves.  
4. Switch off the heat and add the asafoetida powder.  
5. Pour the seasoning over the sambar and mix well.  
6. Cover for a few minutes to retain the aroma.  
7. Garnish with chopped coriander leaves, if desired.

### **Notes**

* Coconut oil gives the sambar its authentic Kerala flavour.  
* You can also use ash gourd, pumpkin, raw banana, or other vegetables of your choice.  
* Add the ladies’ fingers and tomatoes later to help prevent them from becoming too mushy.  
* Adjust the tamarind according to its sourness and your preference.  
* For 8 servings, use a large enough pot to allow the sambar to simmer without overflowing.  
* Serve hot with rice, idli, dosa, or chapati.

# **Pumpkin and Cowpeas Curry (Pumpkin Erissery) – Chickoo’s Favourite**

A traditional Kerala-style pumpkin erissery made with red cowpeas, coconut, and cumin, finished with golden-roasted coconut and a fragrant coconut oil tempering. A favourite dish served during the Onam sadya.

**Medium • 50–60 mins • Main Course • Veg • Serve hot**

### **Ingredients**

**For the curry**

* ½ kg pumpkin, cut into bite-sized pieces  
* 125 g red cowpeas (vanpayar / unakkapayar), soaked overnight  
* ¼ tbsp turmeric powder  
* 1½ tbsp red chilli powder  
* 1 tbsp black pepper powder  
* Salt to taste  
* Water, as needed  
* 1 small piece jaggery (optional, if the pumpkin is not sweet)

**For grinding**

* Grated coconut from ½ coconut (about 4 handfuls)  
* 1 tbsp cumin seeds  
* A little water, as needed

**For tempering**

* Grated coconut from ½ coconut (about 4 handfuls)  
* Mustard seeds, as needed  
* 2 dried red chillies, each cut into 2 pieces  
* ½ tbsp black pepper powder  
* Curry leaves, a few sprigs  
* Coconut oil, as needed  
* Ghee, a little

### **Method**

**1\. Cook the cowpeas**

1. Drain the soaked cowpeas and transfer them to a pressure cooker.  
2. Add salt and enough water to cover the beans by about 2–3 cm.  
3. Cook on high heat until the first whistle, then reduce to medium heat and cook for another 5 minutes.  
4. Allow the pressure to release naturally. Check that the beans are tender.

**2\. Prepare the coconut paste**

1. Grind the grated coconut and cumin seeds with a little water into a fine paste.  
2. Set aside.

**3\. Cook the pumpkin**

1. Place the pumpkin pieces and cooked cowpeas in a pan.  
2. Add the turmeric powder, red chilli powder, black pepper powder, salt, and a little water.  
3. Mix well, cover, and cook until the pumpkin becomes soft. Add water only as needed, as the pumpkin releases moisture while cooking.  
4. Lightly mash some of the pumpkin pieces and mix well to create a semi-dry gravy.

**4\. Add the coconut paste**

1. Add the ground coconut-cumin paste and mix thoroughly.  
2. Simmer over medium heat for a few minutes until the flavours blend and the curry reaches a thick, semi-dry consistency.  
3. Add a small piece of jaggery if the pumpkin lacks sweetness.  
4. Switch off the heat.

**5\. Prepare the roasted coconut tempering**

1. Heat coconut oil in a pan.  
2. Add the mustard seeds and allow them to splutter.  
3. Add the dried red chillies and curry leaves. Fry briefly.  
4. Add the grated coconut and roast, stirring frequently, until golden brown.  
5. Push the roasted coconut mixture towards the sides of the pan and add a little ghee to the centre.  
6. Add the black pepper powder and mix well.  
7. Switch off the heat.

**6\. Finish the curry**

1. Pour the roasted coconut tempering over the pumpkin and cowpeas curry.  
2. Mix gently and serve hot with Kerala rice.

### **Notes**

* This dish is traditionally known as **Pumpkin Erissery**, a classic Kerala Onam sadya curry.  
* Coconut oil gives the curry its authentic Kerala flavour, while the roasted coconut adds a delicious nutty aroma.  
* Adjust the chilli and pepper powders to your preferred spice level.  
* The curry should be thick and semi-dry rather than watery.  
* If using dried cowpeas, soaking them overnight helps them cook evenly.  
* Jaggery is optional; use it only if the pumpkin is not naturally sweet.

# **Kurukku Kalan (Kerala-Style Thick Yoghurt Curry)**

A traditional Kerala sadya curry made with raw banana, elephant yam, coconut, and thick curd, simmered until rich and creamy.

**Medium • 40–50 mins • Side Dish • Veg • Serve warm**

### **Ingredients**

* 1 raw banana, peeled and cubed  
* 1 cup elephant yam (chena), cubed  
* ¼ tsp turmeric powder  
* ½ tsp red chilli powder  
* ½ tsp black pepper powder  
* Salt to taste  
* A little water  
* 1½ cups thick curd (yoghurt)

**For grinding**

* 1 cup grated coconut  
* 1 tsp cumin seeds  
* 2–3 green chillies

**For tempering**

* 2 tbsp coconut oil or other cooking oil  
* 1 tsp mustard seeds  
* ¼ tsp fenugreek seeds  
* 2 dried red chillies  
* 1 sprig curry leaves

### **Method**

1. Peel and cube the raw banana and elephant yam.  
2. Place them in a pan with turmeric powder, red chilli powder, black pepper powder, salt, and a little water.  
3. Cover and cook until the vegetables are tender and most of the water has evaporated.  
4. Grind the grated coconut, cumin seeds, and green chillies into a smooth paste.  
5. Add the paste to the cooked vegetables and simmer for 3–4 minutes.  
6. Beat the curd until smooth. Lower the heat and gradually add the curd, stirring gently.  
7. Cook over very low heat until the curry thickens. Do not allow it to boil vigorously after adding the curd.  
8. For the tempering, heat coconut oil in a small pan.  
9. Add the mustard seeds and allow them to splutter. Add fenugreek seeds, dried red chillies, and curry leaves.  
10. Pour the tempering over the kalan and mix gently.

### **Notes**

* Traditional kalan is thick, slightly sour, and peppery.  
* Use thick curd for the best consistency.  
* The curry thickens further as it cools.  
* Serve with rice as part of a Kerala sadya.

# **Kerala Koottu Curry (Black Chickpeas and Vegetables)**

A traditional Kerala sadya dish made with black chickpeas, raw banana, yam, and coconut, finished with roasted coconut for a nutty flavour.

**Medium • 50–60 mins • Side Dish • Veg • Serve hot**

### **Ingredients**

* ½ cup black chickpeas (kadala), soaked overnight  
* 1 medium raw banana, peeled and cubed  
* 1 cup elephant yam (chena), cubed  
* 2 green chillies, slit  
* ¼ tsp turmeric powder  
* ½ tsp red chilli powder  
* ½ tsp black pepper powder  
* Salt to taste  
* Water, as needed

**For grinding**

* 1 cup grated coconut  
* 1 tsp cumin seeds  
* 3–4 dried red chillies

**For roasting**

* 3 tbsp grated coconut

**For tempering**

* 2 tbsp coconut oil  
* 1 tsp mustard seeds  
* 2 dried red chillies  
* 1 sprig curry leaves

### **Method**

1. Drain the soaked black chickpeas. Pressure-cook with enough water until tender. Allow the pressure to release naturally.  
2. Place the raw banana and yam in a pan with turmeric powder, red chilli powder, black pepper powder, salt, and enough water to cook.  
3. Cover and cook until the vegetables are tender.  
4. Grind the grated coconut, cumin seeds, and dried red chillies into a coarse paste, using as little water as possible.  
5. Add the cooked chickpeas and ground coconut paste to the vegetables. Mix well.  
6. Simmer for 5–7 minutes until the flavours blend and the gravy becomes semi-thick.  
7. In a separate pan, dry-roast 3 tbsp grated coconut until golden brown.  
8. Add the roasted coconut to the curry and mix well.  
9. Heat coconut oil in a small pan. Add mustard seeds and allow them to splutter.  
10. Add the dried red chillies and curry leaves. Fry briefly and pour the tempering over the curry.  
11. Mix gently and serve hot.

### **Notes**

* Adjust the thickness with a little hot water if needed. The curry should be semi-thick.  
* Roasted coconut gives koottu curry its characteristic nutty flavour.  
* Make sure the black chickpeas are fully tender before combining them with the vegetables.  
* Serve with rice, olan, avial, and pappadam as part of a Kerala sadya.

# **Kerala Cabbage Thoran**

A simple Kerala-style cabbage stir-fry with grated coconut, green chillies, curry leaves, and coconut oil.

**Easy • 20 mins • Side Dish • Veg • Serve hot**

### **Ingredients**

* 3 cups finely chopped cabbage  
* ½ cup grated coconut  
* 2 green chillies, chopped  
* ½ onion, finely chopped, or 3 shallots  
* ¼ tsp turmeric powder  
* ½ tsp mustard seeds  
* 2 dried red chillies  
* 1 sprig curry leaves  
* 2 tbsp coconut oil or other cooking oil  
* Salt to taste

### **Method**

1. Place the chopped cabbage in a bowl. Add turmeric powder and salt, then mix well.  
2. Heat coconut oil in a pan.  
3. Add the mustard seeds and allow them to splutter.  
4. Add the dried red chillies and curry leaves. Fry briefly.  
5. Add the chopped onion or shallots and green chillies. Sauté until softened.  
6. Add the cabbage and mix well.  
7. Cover and cook over low heat for 5–7 minutes, stirring occasionally. Add a small splash of water only if necessary.  
8. Add the grated coconut and mix gently.  
9. Cook uncovered for another 2 minutes, until the cabbage is tender but retains a little bite.  
10. Serve hot.

### **Notes**

* Do not overcook the cabbage; thoran tastes best with a little crunch.  
* You can add a pinch of cumin seeds to the grated coconut for extra flavour.  
* Coconut oil gives the dish an authentic Kerala taste.  
* Serve with rice, sambar, rasam, or curd curry.

# **Spicy Potato Fry** 

A simple and spicy potato fry with garlic, red chilli powder and curry leaves. A delicious accompaniment to rice, dal curry or curd rice.

**Easy • 20–25 mins • Side Dish • Veg**

### **Ingredients**

* 3 large potatoes  
* 3 garlic cloves, crushed  
* 1 tbsp red chilli powder  
* ¼ tsp turmeric powder  
* ¾ tsp salt, or to taste  
* 1 tbsp oil  
* 1 tsp mustard seeds  
* A few curry leaves

### **Method**

1. Peel the potatoes and cut them into small, even-sized cubes.  
2. Heat the oil in a pan over medium heat.  
3. Add the mustard seeds and let them splutter.  
4. Add the crushed garlic and curry leaves. Sauté until the raw smell of the garlic disappears.  
5. Add the chopped potatoes and fry for about 5 minutes, stirring occasionally.  
6. Add the salt, turmeric powder and red chilli powder. Mix well so the spices coat the potatoes evenly.  
7. Continue frying over medium-low heat until the potatoes are cooked through and the chilli powder is roasted without burning.  
8. Stir occasionally to prevent sticking. Switch off the heat once the potatoes are tender but still hold their shape.

### **Notes**

* Cut the potatoes into small, even cubes so they cook uniformly.  
* Adjust the red chilli powder according to your preferred spice level.  
* Avoid overcooking or stirring too vigorously, as the potatoes may break apart.  
* For a crispier finish, cook uncovered over medium-low heat, stirring occasionally.

# **Green Chilli Pachadi (Mulaka Pachadi)**

A spicy, tangy and sweet Kerala-style chilli pachadi flavoured with roasted spices, tamarind and jaggery.

**Easy • 30–40 mins • Pickle/Pachadi • Veg**

### **Ingredients**

* 15 green chillies  
* 150 g jaggery (adjust according to taste)  
* 150 g tamarind  
* 3 tbsp sesame seeds  
* 1 tbsp mustard seeds (for grinding)  
* 1 tsp fenugreek seeds  
* 1 tsp cumin seeds  
* 1 tsp turmeric powder  
* 1½ tsp red chilli powder  
* 1 tbsp sesame oil  
* 1 sprig curry leaves  
* ¾ tsp salt, or to taste

### **For Tempering**

* 1 tbsp oil  
* 1 tbsp mustard seeds  
* 2 dried red chillies  
* A few curry leaves

### **Method**

1. Soak the tamarind in warm water and extract a thick tamarind juice. Set aside.  
2. Melt the jaggery in a little hot water and strain to remove impurities.  
3. Dry-roast the sesame seeds, mustard seeds, cumin seeds and fenugreek seeds separately over a low flame until aromatic. Allow them to cool and grind into a fine powder.  
4. Wash and wipe the green chillies dry. Slit them lengthwise and cut them into long pieces.  
5. Heat sesame oil in a pan. Add the green chillies and curry leaves. Sauté over low to medium heat until the raw smell reduces and the chillies soften slightly.  
6. Add turmeric powder, red chilli powder and salt. Mix well.  
7. Pour in the tamarind extract and add the melted jaggery. Cook until the mixture begins to thicken.  
8. Add the ground spice powder and mix well. Simmer for a few minutes until the pachadi becomes thick and glossy.  
9. For the tempering, heat oil in a small pan. Add mustard seeds and let them splutter. Add the dried red chillies and curry leaves.  
10. Pour the tempering over the pachadi and mix gently.

### **Notes**

* Adjust the jaggery according to the sourness of the tamarind and the heat of the chillies.  
* Sesame oil gives the pachadi a traditional flavour, but other cooking oils can be used.  
* Store in a clean, dry, airtight bottle and refrigerate. Use a clean, dry spoon.

# **Raw Papaya Pachadi**

A creamy Kerala-style pachadi made with cooked raw papaya, coconut, green chillies and curd.

**Easy • 20–25 mins • Side Dish • Veg**

### **Ingredients**

* 1½ cups raw green papaya, peeled and finely chopped  
* ½ cup grated coconut  
* 4 green chillies  
* 1 tsp mustard seeds (for grinding)  
* ½ cup curd (yoghurt)  
* Salt to taste  
* 1 tsp sugar (optional)

### **For Tempering**

* 1 tsp coconut oil or other cooking oil  
* ½ tsp mustard seeds  
* 1 dried red chilli  
* 1 sprig curry leaves

### **Method**

1. Place the chopped raw papaya in a pan with a little water and salt. Cook until soft but not mushy.  
2. Grind the grated coconut into a coarse paste. Add the green chillies and mustard seeds, then pulse two or three times until combined. Avoid grinding too smoothly.  
3. Add the coconut mixture to the cooked papaya and mix well. Cook for another 2–3 minutes.  
4. Switch off the heat and allow the mixture to cool slightly.  
5. Add the curd and mix gently until well combined. The pachadi should have a semi-thick, creamy consistency.  
6. Heat the oil in a small pan. Add the mustard seeds and let them splutter. Add the dried red chilli and curry leaves.  
7. Pour the tempering over the pachadi and mix gently.

### **Notes**

* Do not boil the pachadi after adding curd, as it may split.  
* Keep the consistency thick and creamy rather than watery.  
* Sugar is optional and can help balance the tanginess.  
* You can substitute the raw papaya with grated beetroot, raw mango, white melon or tomato. Cooking time may vary depending on the ingredient used.

# **Mixed Vegetable Pickle**

A spicy, tangy mixed vegetable pickle combining cauliflower, carrot, raw mango and aromatic Kerala-style spices.

**Easy • 30–40 mins • Pickle • Veg**

### **Ingredients**

* 3 cauliflower florets  
* 2 large carrots  
* 1 large raw mango (fresh or frozen)  
* 1 large piece of ginger  
* 2 whole bulbs of garlic  
* 16 large green chillies (milder varieties, if preferred)  
* ¾ cup vinegar  
* Salt to taste  
* ¾ tbsp fenugreek seeds  
* ½ tsp cumin seeds  
* 1 tbsp mustard seeds  
* ½ tsp asafoetida (hing)  
* 4 dried red chillies  
* 2 sprigs curry leaves  
* 2 tbsp gingelly (sesame) oil  
* ¾ tbsp turmeric powder  
* 4 tbsp red chilli powder

### **Method**

1. Cut the cauliflower, carrots and raw mango into small pieces. Slice the ginger and green chillies. Peel the garlic.  
2. Place the vegetables, ginger, garlic and green chillies in a bowl. Add salt and mix well.  
3. Dry-roast the fenugreek seeds and cumin seeds separately until aromatic. Allow them to cool and grind into a fine powder.  
4. Heat the gingelly oil in a pan. Add the mustard seeds and let them splutter.  
5. Add the dried red chillies and curry leaves.  
6. Reduce the heat to low. Add the turmeric powder and red chilli powder, stirring carefully so the spices do not burn.  
7. Add the prepared vegetables, ground fenugreek-cumin powder and asafoetida. Mix thoroughly.  
8. Pour in the vinegar and mix well.  
9. Bring the mixture to a gentle boil and cook for 2–3 minutes, or until the vegetables begin to soften but still retain some bite.  
10. Switch off the heat and allow the pickle to cool completely before transferring it to a clean, dry container.

### **Notes**

* Adjust the salt, vinegar and chilli powder according to taste.  
* Cut the vegetables into similar-sized pieces so they cook evenly.  
* Use gingelly oil for a traditional flavour, or substitute another cooking oil.  
* Store in the refrigerator and always use a clean, dry spoon. Homemade vegetable pickles are not automatically shelf-stable; refrigerate promptly and do not rely on the vinegar alone to preserve them.

# **Raw Mango Curry (Mango Pachadi)**

A tangy, spicy Kerala-style raw mango curry with crushed dried red chillies, mustard seeds and a touch of curd.

**Easy • 15 mins \+ overnight resting • Side Dish • Veg**

### **Ingredients**

* 1 raw mango  
* 3 dried red chillies, soaked in water  
* 1 sprig curry leaves  
* ½ tbsp mustard seeds (for grinding)  
* A pinch of asafoetida (hing)  
* 2 shallots, finely chopped  
* 1 tbsp curd  
* Salt to taste  
* Oil, as needed for tempering  
* A little mustard seed for tempering

### **Method**

1. Cut the raw mango into small pieces, leaving the skin on.  
2. Mix the mango pieces with salt and leave overnight to absorb the flavour.  
3. Drain the soaked red chillies. Coarsely crush them with the mustard seeds.  
4. Heat oil in a pan. Add a little mustard seed and let it splutter.  
5. Add the chopped shallots and sauté until golden brown. Add the curry leaves.  
6. Reduce the heat and add the coarsely ground chilli-mustard mixture. Sauté until the raw smell disappears, taking care not to burn it.  
7. Add the asafoetida and mix well.  
8. Switch off the heat and pour the seasoning over the salted mango pieces.  
9. Check the salt, add the curd and mix gently.

### **Notes**

* The overnight resting helps the mango absorb the salt and develop flavour.  
* Adjust the number of dried red chillies according to your preferred spice level.  
* Add the curd only after the seasoning has cooled slightly.  
* Keep refrigerated and use a clean, dry spoon.

# **Lemon Curry**

A tangy and spicy lemon curry with mustard seeds, fenugreek and curry leaves, balanced with a pinch of sugar.

**Easy • 15–20 mins • Side Dish/Pickle • Veg**

### **Ingredients**

* 1 lemon  
* 2 green chillies, chopped  
* 1½ tsp red chilli powder  
* 1 tbsp oil  
* ½ tsp mustard seeds  
* ½ tsp fenugreek seeds  
* ½ tsp salt, or to taste  
* A few curry leaves  
* 3 tbsp water  
* A pinch of sugar

### **Method**

1. Wash the lemon and cut it into small pieces, removing any seeds.  
2. Heat the oil in a pan. Add the mustard seeds and let them splutter.  
3. Add the fenugreek seeds and sauté briefly, taking care not to burn them.  
4. Add the curry leaves.

9. Switch off the heat and allow the curry to cool before serving.

# **Salmon in Lemon Brown Butter**

# **Parmesan-Crusted Chicken**

## **Ingredients**

* 2 boneless, skinless chicken breasts  
* ½ cup grated Parmesan cheese  
* ½ cup breadcrumbs (panko for extra crispiness)  
* 1 egg  
* 2 tbsp all-purpose flour  
* 2 cloves garlic, minced  
* ½ tsp black pepper  
* ½ tsp salt  
* 1 tsp dried oregano or Italian seasoning  
* 2–3 tbsp olive oil or butter

## **Method**

### **Prepare the Chicken**

1. Slice each chicken breast horizontally to make thinner pieces, or pound the breasts to an even thickness.  
2. Prepare three shallow bowls:  
   * **Bowl 1:** Mix the flour, salt, and black pepper.  
   * **Bowl 2:** Beat the egg with the minced garlic.  
   * **Bowl 3:** Mix the Parmesan cheese, breadcrumbs, and oregano or Italian seasoning.  
3. Coat each chicken piece in the flour mixture, dip it into the egg mixture, then press it into the Parmesan-breadcrumb mixture until well coated.

### **Pan-Fry**

4. Heat the olive oil or butter in a frying pan over medium heat.  
5. Add the coated chicken and cook for about 4–5 minutes on each side, until golden brown and crisp.  
6. Check that the chicken is fully cooked in the centre, with no pink remaining. The safe internal temperature is 74°C.

### **Optional Oven Finish**

7. For thicker pieces, transfer the pan-fried chicken to a preheated oven at 180°C and bake for another 8–10 minutes, or until fully cooked.

## **Notes**

* Panko breadcrumbs give the coating extra crunch.  
* Serve with mashed potatoes, garlic rice, and a fresh vegetable salad.  
* Adjust the cooking time according to the thickness of the chicken.

**Fruit Custard**  
Creamy chilled custard loaded with fresh seasonal fruits.  
**Easy • 20 mins • Dessert • Veg • Chilled**

### **Ingredients**

* 2 cups milk  
* 2 tbsp custard powder (vanilla)  
* 3–4 tbsp sugar (adjust to taste)  
* 1–1.5 cups mixed fruits, chopped

### **Method**

1. Take ¼ cup cold milk and mix in the custard powder until smooth (no lumps).  
2. Bring the remaining milk to a gentle boil.  
3. Add sugar and stir until dissolved.  
4. Lower the heat and slowly pour in the custard mixture, stirring continuously.  
5. Cook on low heat for 3–5 minutes until it thickens.  
6. Turn off heat and let it cool.  
7. Refrigerate for 1–2 hours.  
8. Add chopped fruits just before serving and mix gently.

### **Notes**

* Best fruits to use: apple, banana, mango, grapes, pomegranate.  
* Avoid citrus fruits like orange or pineapple as they can curdle the custard or make it taste bitter.  
* Add fruits only after the custard is fully chilled to keep them fresh and prevent wateriness.

# **Caramel Custard Pudding**

## **Ingredients**

**For the custard**

* 2 cups milk  
* 5 eggs  
* 10 tbsp sugar  
* 1 tbsp vanilla essence

**For the caramel**

* 8 tbsp sugar  
* A little water, just enough to help dissolve the sugar

## **Method**

### **Prepare the Caramel**

1. Add the sugar and a little water to a saucepan.  
2. Heat over medium heat until the sugar dissolves and turns golden brown.  
3. Carefully pour the caramel into the pudding mould, tilting the mould to coat the bottom. Set aside.

### **Prepare the Custard**

4. In a bowl, beat the eggs gently.  
5. Add the sugar and mix until dissolved.  
6. Add the vanilla essence and milk. Mix well.  
7. Pour the custard mixture into the caramel-lined mould. For a smoother pudding, strain the mixture before pouring.

### **Steam the Pudding**

8. Place the mould in a steamer and steam over low to medium heat until the custard is set. The time will depend on the mould size.  
9. Insert a toothpick or skewer into the centre. It should come out mostly clean, with the custard set.  
10. Switch off the heat and allow the pudding to cool.  
11. Run a knife around the edges of the mould.  
12. Place a serving plate over the mould and carefully invert it.  
13. Refrigerate until thoroughly chilled before serving.

## **Notes**

* Be careful when handling hot caramel, as it can cause severe burns.  
* Avoid beating too much air into the eggs to prevent bubbles in the pudding.  
* For a smooth texture, steam gently rather than allowing the water to boil vigorously.

# **Marble Pudding**

A beautiful two-tone milk pudding with delicate vanilla and chocolate swirls.

**Easy • 20 mins preparation • 2–3 hrs chilling • Dessert • Veg • Chilled**

### **Ingredients**

* 1 litre milk  
* 10 g china grass (agar-agar)  
* ½ cup water, for soaking the china grass  
* 5–6 tbsp sugar (adjust to taste)  
* 1 tsp vanilla essence  
* 1 tbsp cocoa powder  
* 3–4 tbsp condensed milk (optional, for richness)

### **Method**

**Prepare the china grass**

1. Cut the china grass into small pieces, if necessary, and soak it in ½ cup water for 10–15 minutes.  
2. Heat gently until completely dissolved, stirring to ensure no strands remain.

**Prepare the milk mixture**

3. Heat the milk, sugar, and condensed milk in a saucepan.  
4. Stir until the sugar dissolves. Avoid boiling vigorously.  
5. Add the dissolved china grass to the hot milk, stirring continuously for 2–3 minutes.

**Create the marble effect**

6. Divide the mixture equally between two bowls.  
7. Add the vanilla essence to one portion.  
8. Mix the cocoa powder with a little warm milk to form a smooth paste, then stir it into the second portion.  
9. Pour a ladleful of the white mixture into a mould, followed by some chocolate mixture.  
10. Continue alternating the two mixtures.  
11. Gently swirl a skewer or knife through the mixture to create a marble pattern. Do not overmix.

**Set the pudding**

12. Allow it to cool at room temperature.  
13. Refrigerate for 2–3 hours, or until completely set.

### **Notes**

* Agar-agar powder can be used instead of china grass; follow the package instructions for the appropriate quantity.  
* Work quickly while the mixtures are still pourable, as agar-agar begins to set as it cools.  
* Do not over-swirl, or the marble pattern may disappear

# **Tender Coconut Pudding**

A refreshing, creamy pudding made with tender coconut water and soft coconut flesh.

**Easy • 20 mins preparation • 2–3 hrs chilling • Dessert • Veg • Chilled**

### **Ingredients**

* 2 cups tender coconut water  
* 1 cup tender coconut flesh (soft malai), chopped or lightly blended  
* 1 cup milk  
* 8–10 g china grass (agar-agar)  
* ½ cup water, for soaking the china grass  
* 4–5 tbsp sugar (adjust to taste)  
* 2–3 tbsp condensed milk (optional)

### **Method**

**Prepare the china grass**

1. Cut the china grass into small pieces, if necessary, and soak it in ½ cup water for 10–15 minutes.

**Prepare the milk mixture**

3. Heat the milk, sugar, and condensed milk in a saucepan.  
4. Stir until the sugar dissolves. There is no need to boil vigorously.  
5. Add the dissolved china grass to the hot milk and stir continuously for 2–3 minutes.

**Add the coconut**

6. Switch off the heat and allow the mixture to cool slightly while remaining liquid.  
7. Stir in the tender coconut water and coconut flesh gently until combined.

**Set the pudding**

9. Allow it to cool at room temperature.  
10. Refrigerate for 2–3 hours, or until set.

# **Rice Payasam**

A traditional, creamy rice pudding simmered in milk and flavoured with cardamom, cashews, and raisins.

**Easy • 40 mins • Dessert • Veg • Serve warm or chilled**

### **Ingredients**

* ½ cup raw rice, preferably small-grain rice  
* 1 litre milk  
* ¾ cup sugar  
* 1 tbsp ghee  
* Cashew nuts, as needed  
* Raisins, as needed  
* A pinch of salt  
* Powdered cardamom, to taste

### **Method**

1. If using basmati rice, pulse it briefly in a mixer to break the grains into smaller pieces. Do not grind it into a powder.  
2. Add the rice and milk to a large, heavy-bottomed pot.  
3. Cook over medium-low heat for about 30 minutes, stirring frequently to prevent the milk from sticking or burning.  
4. Continue cooking until the rice is soft and the payasam reaches your preferred consistency.  
5. Add the sugar and stir until dissolved.  
6. Add a pinch of salt and powdered cardamom. Mix well.  
7. Heat the ghee in a small pan.  
8. Fry the cashews until golden, then add the raisins and fry until they puff up.  
9. Pour the fried nuts, raisins, and ghee over the payasam. Mix gently and serve.

### **Notes**

* Use a large pot, as milk can rise quickly when heated.  
* Stir frequently, especially near the bottom of the pot.  
* The payasam thickens further as it cools. Add a little warm milk if needed before serving.

# **Vermicelli Payasam (Semiya Payasam)**

A comforting Indian milk dessert made with roasted vermicelli, cardamom, cashews, and raisins.

**Easy • 25–30 mins • Dessert • Veg • Serve warm or chilled**

### **Ingredients**

* 1 cup vermicelli (semiya)  
* 1 litre milk  
* 3–4 tbsp sugar (adjust to taste)  
* 2 tbsp ghee  
* 10–12 cashew nuts  
* 10–15 raisins  
* 3–4 cardamom pods, crushed  
* A few saffron strands (optional)

### **Method**

1. Heat 1 tbsp ghee in a pan.  
2. Add the vermicelli and roast over low heat until lightly golden. Skip this step if using pre-roasted vermicelli.  
3. In another pot, bring the milk to a boil, stirring occasionally to prevent it from sticking.  
4. Add the roasted vermicelli to the milk.  
5. Cook over medium-low heat for 8–10 minutes, or until the vermicelli is soft.  
6. Add the sugar and crushed cardamom. Stir well and simmer for another 3–5 minutes.  
7. In a small pan, heat the remaining ghee.  
8. Fry the cashews until golden, then add the raisins and fry until they puff up.  
9. Add the fried nuts, raisins, and ghee to the payasam. Mix well.  
10. Add saffron strands, if using, and serve.

### **Notes**

* Adjust the sugar to taste.  
* Stir frequently while cooking the vermicelli to prevent it from sticking to the bottom.  
* The payasam thickens as it cools. Add a little warm milk if you prefer a thinner consistency.

# **Split Green Gram Payasam (Moong Dal Payasam)**

A traditional, rich payasam made with roasted split green gram, jaggery, coconut milk, and aromatic spices.

**Easy • 40–50 mins • Dessert • Veg • Serve warm**

### **Ingredients**

* 1 cup split green gram (moong dal)  
* 4 cups lukewarm water, for cooking the dal  
* 300–400 g jaggery (adjust to taste)  
* 1 cup water, for melting the jaggery  
* ½ cup thick coconut milk  
* 1 tbsp coconut milk, to add to the cooking water  
* 2–3 tbsp ghee  
* 10 cashew nuts  
* 10 raisins  
* ½ tsp cardamom powder  
* ¼ tsp dry ginger powder

### **Method**

**Cook the dal**

1. Dry-roast the split green gram in a pan over medium-low heat until the raw smell disappears. Do not let it turn brown.  
2. Transfer the roasted dal to a pressure cooker.  
3. Add the 4 cups of water and 1 tbsp coconut milk.  
4. Pressure-cook until the first whistle, then cook for another 5 minutes over low heat.  
5. Switch off the heat and allow the pressure to release naturally.  
6. Open the cooker and check that the dal is soft.

**Prepare the jaggery**

7. Melt the jaggery in 1 cup water over low heat.  
8. Strain the melted jaggery syrup to remove any impurities.  
9. Add the jaggery syrup to the cooked dal.  
10. Simmer for about 5 minutes, stirring gently.

**Finish the payasam**

11. Reduce the heat and stir in the thick coconut milk.  
12. Add the cardamom powder and dry ginger powder. Mix well.  
13. Heat the ghee in a small pan.  
14. Fry the cashews until golden, then add the raisins and fry until they puff up.  
15. Pour the fried nuts, raisins, and ghee into the payasam. Mix gently and serve warm.

### **Notes**

* Do not brown the dal while roasting, as this can change the flavour.  
* Strain the jaggery syrup before adding it to the dal.  
* After adding the thick coconut milk, heat gently and avoid vigorous boiling.  
* Adjust the jaggery according to your preferred sweetness.

**Spice Mixes/Masala Powders**

## **Garam Masala**

Aromatic homemade spice blend roasted and freshly ground.  
**Easy • 15 mins • Veg • Spice Mix • Homemade**

### **Ingredients**

* 50 g fennel seeds (about 1/2 cup)  
* 20 g cardamom (about 1/4 cup)  
* 20 g cloves  
* 20 g cinnamon

### **Method**

1. Dry roast all the spices in a frying pan on low heat until aromatic.  
2. Let the spices cool completely.  
3. Grind into a fine powder.  
4. Store in an airtight container.

### **Notes**

* Store in the freezer to maintain freshness and aroma for longer.  
* Ensure spices are completely cool before grinding to avoid moisture.

## **Tandoori Masala**

A bold, smoky spice blend for classic tandoori-style dishes.  
**Medium • 20 mins • Veg • Spice Mix • Homemade**

### **Ingredients**

* 1/4 cup coriander seeds (or 3 tbsp coriander powder)  
* 1 tbsp cumin seeds  
* 2 tsp peppercorns  
* 10 green cardamom  
* 2 black cardamom (optional)  
* 2 inch cinnamon piece  
* 2 pieces mace  
* 1 tsp nutmeg powder  
* 10 Kashmiri red chillies (or 3 tbsp chilli powder)  
* 2 tsp kasoori methi (dry fenugreek leaves)

### **Method**

1. Dry roast coriander seeds, cumin seeds, peppercorns, cloves, cardamom, cinnamon, and mace on low heat for 2–3 minutes until fragrant.  
2. If using coriander powder or chilli powder instead of whole spices, add them  after switching off the flame.  
3. Add kasuri methi and nutmeg powder. Let everything cool completely.  
4. Grind into a fine powder.  
5. Mix well and store in an airtight container.

### **Notes**  

* Adjust chilli based on spice and colour preference.  
* Store in a cool, dry place or refrigerate for longer freshness.
